# Hero Logo Transition — Material Reference Frames

**Status:** Reference material only. Not approved design, not production assets, no
implementation authority. Generated 24 July 2026 via Codex image generation before that
account lapsed, and salvaged here.

**What these are for:** a **material and lighting reference** for the hero
gold ⇄ blue-platinum transition described in
`project-intelligence/live-work/hero-logo-transition-concept.md`. The actual
implementation route is Three.js on the vector master (`../c2b-logo-gold-hero.svg`) —
same geometry throughout, animated material properties only. These frames show what the
target states and midpoints should look like.

They are **not** production assets: backgrounds are baked in (no alpha), and the
geometry in some frames does not match the master.

---

## The transition sequence (4 frames, in order)

| Frame | File | State |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `c2b-transition-1-platinum-blue.png` | Full cool platinum/blue. Icy white core along the stroke, cool blue edge definition. The "code" end state. |
| 2 | `c2b-transition-2-warm-enters.png` | Warm light **enters at the edges and highlights first** while the body stays cool. Warm bounce appears on the surface below. |
| 3 | `c2b-transition-3-gold-dominant.png` | Gold now dominant along the stroke; cool blue survives only in the shadowed interior and lower flank. Strong warm floor bounce. |
| 4 | `c2b-transition-4-full-gold.png` | Full gold. Blue reduced to a residual cool core. Warm environmental bounce at its strongest. |

### The structural observation that matters

**This is not a hue lerp.** The warm light enters at the **edges and specular
highlights** first while the body remains cool, then floods inward. It reads as a
*material and lighting response* — the environment changing around a fixed object —
rather than a colour fade applied to a flat shape.

That matches the existing coupled-value rule (D-031/D-032, R-016/R-017): light should
**modify an existing derived value**, not sit on top as an independent overlay. A
Three.js implementation should therefore drive environment/light colour and material
response, **not** interpolate a base colour.

Note also the **floor bounce tracks the material state** across all four frames — the
surface below picks up the warm light as the logo warms. Effects caused by the world,
not layered on top of it.

## Known gap

A **teal/copper** variant was also generated but was **not saved** before the Codex
account was closed. It showed a more saturated, duotone treatment. Judged more
saturated than the brand line permits (*"metallic, refined, controlled — not loud, not
flashy"*), so its loss is not material; recorded here only so the gap is known rather
than discovered later.
