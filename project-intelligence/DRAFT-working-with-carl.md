## What this file is

`mission-overview.md` is about the **company**. This is about **Carl** — the
person behind the decisions. The thinking underneath a choice, what he is
reaching for *emotionally*, the resonance he wants, and how he moves through
the creative process.

The aim, in his words: **two musicians who have played together a long time.**
They know each other's style, phrasing, behaviours — so a glance or a few notes
is enough; the rest is understood. The goal here is that kind of shorthand.
Given enough of the pattern, a short prompt plus a reference should let the
builder make a genuinely *informed* guess at where Carl is going — not a cold
one.

An honest limit, recorded up front: this makes the builder's inferences **more
informed**, not *felt*. The builder can read the metaphor and reconstruct the
structure — "technique held while emotion goes all the way" — but does not
experience the Adagio. The file sharpens the guess. It does not make the guess
Carl's. Ask it to do the first, never the second.

## How Carl transmits intent — "show, don't tell"

He rarely hands over a thesis on what a feature should feel like. He **shows** a
reference and tags it with a short compass bearing:

- The reference carries the *substance* (the T1000 edge, the Adagio's second
  modulation, the performer adjusting in the wings, Comfortably Numb's second
  solo).
- A short phrase carries the *orientation* — e.g. **"technique meets emotion,"**
  **"from a guy selling front doors."**

So when Carl gives a metaphor or analogy, it will usually arrive with a phrase
like that attached. **Read the pairing, not just the reference.** The phrase is
the calibration data — it tells you which part of the reference is load-bearing
and which is incidental. (This is itself the "show don't tell" ethos — the same
one the website is meant to embody.)

## Recurring tells in the pattern (observed, will grow)

- **He chooses the *live* version of everything.** Live recordings over studio
  cuts; a logo that is *waiting* and *notices* you over one that plays a
  finished render; the brand becoming *active* rather than playing back. The
  through-line is presence and risk happening *now*, once — not a polished
  artifact. When in doubt, lean toward the living, responsive reading.
- **Cause over decoration.** Effects should be *caused by the world*, not laid
  on top of it. Light spilling onto text because the logo moved; sparks as the
  *cost* of a transition, not ornament. This matches the coupled-value rule
  already in the decisions record (D-031/D-032) — it is a personal instinct,
  not only a technical one.
- **Restraint, then release.** The quiet sets up the loud. Debussy's *Clair de
  Lune* over Beethoven's Fifth: the flourish lands *because* everything before
  it held back. He will discard something for being "cheesy" (overt,
  self-announcing) and keep the same mechanism when it is earned and placed.
- **The creative process moves, and he expects it to.** *"Rarely does what I
  hear in my head come out the speakers at the end."* Ideas change on the fly;
  inspiration arrives mid-process. The unexpected turns (the hero and
  client-info sections going somewhere neither of us predicted) are **the
  pattern, not noise.** Hold his stated direction *loosely* — it is direction
  of travel, provisional by design.
- **The stakes are personal and high.** *"This is my front door, from a guy
  selling front doors."* First impressions, reputation — a lifetime to build,
  seconds to destroy. "It has to be killer" is a real specification, not
  enthusiasm.

## The intent-and-chunk tension (one worked example of the method)

The reason Carl gives the *whole* emotional arc of a feature — not just a flat
spec — is so the builder knows what to **protect** and what to **sacrifice**
when a real constraint bites. Without the why, the first hard trade is a guess,
and a wrong guess builds something technically correct and emotionally dead.

But the whole arc creates the opposite risk: looking **too far ahead** —
pre-building a later beat, adding abstractions a future chunk might need,
hesitating on the current chunk because of something downstream. The band knows
the whole song; they are recording verse one.

Working resolution (not final): **the intent governs, the chunk executes.** The
arc is context for judgement — read-only with respect to scope. It shapes *how*
the current bite-sized chunk is built; it never authorises work on a later beat,
pre-optimisation, or hesitation. Guard specifically against *helpful*
forward-creep ("verse one would be cleaner if I set up the breakout now") — that
looks like good engineering while quietly expanding scope. If the current chunk
and the arc seem to conflict: **stop and ask**, do not reach forward.

Epigraph (agreed, correctly attributed to **Qui-Gon Jinn**, not Yoda):

> "Keep your concentration here and now, where it belongs."
> — "But not at the expense of the moment."

Presence is not the absence of the arc. The arc is *why the moment matters*;
the moment is the *only place the arc gets built*. Verse one, played like it
knows the solo is coming — but played, all the same, as verse one.
