## FILM ONLY, NOT IN THE SCENE

- **Before O1: the discussion** (D1–D6 in `beat-list.md`): the direction, the references, the concept, the details.
  **First (Carl, 25 November: *"the first thing is discussion. utilising threejs to build a scene and to tell a story. Quick non
  tech description of threejs as opposed to HTML/CSS"*), the Builder's draft. Carl on lines 1, 3–5: *"Thats a good description.
  It plays into the modern way of building websites."* (a verdict, not worded as an approval). Line 2 from his next words: *"Websites
  are information dumps. We can either tell a clients story with a flat, document type way or get visually more creative, more
  engaging."* (the Builder's wording, NOT yet seen):
  1. Most websites are built with HTML and CSS. Think of a printed page: words, pictures and boxes, laid out flat.
  2. Many websites end up as information dumps. A client's story can be told the flat way, like a document. Or it can be told visually, in a way that holds people's attention.
  3. three.js is different. It builds a 3D world inside the page, with objects, materials, lights and a camera. Closer to a film set or a video game than a page.
  4. Like a game, it's drawn live as you watch. Things can move, melt, get wet and catch the light. So instead of just showing a page, we can tell a story.
  5. We build every object the same way. First its shape, the mesh. Then what it's made of, the material. Then the light that shows it. Each step is approved before the next.
  6. Our hero isn't a picture. It's a scene, made entirely in code, that plays out when you arrive.
