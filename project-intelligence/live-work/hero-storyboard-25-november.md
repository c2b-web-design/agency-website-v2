## An outside OPINION on the scene (25 November; asked by Carl; taste and direction, Carl's to use or discard)

- **Works:** the story (molten → shaped → cooled → worked under a cloth → unveiled gold on marble) fits the headline; the blobs
  over the lava floor the most alive part; the gold on marble the image to remember; holding still at the end right for a hero.
- **Falls down:** (1) the cloth section too long and shapeless (~41 s by the printed times, STORY 00:00:32:20–00:01:13:11, about
  half the scene, from one fixed view) — "fiddling rather than craftsmanship", made worse by the logo and cloth passing through
  each other; (2) ~9 s with nothing 3D at the start (visitors scroll); (3) the colour "going backwards" after gold (CHECKED:
  lighting by angle, not the material — the gold arrives once and stays; the perception stands: the showcase lights);
  (4) the scene and the text compete (the logo over "built" is the approved lean, R-103; the clipped text was its side panel);
  (5) the upside-down logo reads "CSP" near the climax (pass 4's 180, approved R-111: a camera question).
- **Its ranking:** shorten the cloth section, one job (cover → gild → unveil); gold once and stays; stop the passing-through;
  movement in the first 2–3 s; keep the 3D clear of the text; the stutters last.
- **Its last point:** 90 s is long for a hero; most visitors see the first 10–20 s and the end: a shorter loop, or SCROLL moving
  the scene forward (structural; `/about`'s desk mark is scroll-driven). Not ruled.
- **The Builder's reading:** most of its fixes are the camera's (phase 5): show each pass's RESULT (the Schrödinger rule hides the
  change, so the viewer needs the after), avoid "CSP" head-on, get close on the blobs, find angles where the gold reads as gold.

## Carl's objection, and the outside answer (25 November): RAISED, NOT CHOSEN

**Carl:** *"The problem comes if its mouse driven. Camera direction comes next and we have built a camera bench and will work out the
shots and timings according to a storyboard. Allowing a visitor to control that is giving them access to control the story and
speed ramp the whole thing."* → scroll or mouse driving the timeline: NOT taken (the visitor would become the editor).

The outside answer's three middle grounds, each with the Builder's reading:
1. **The visitor triggers the next part; the authored speed plays it.** Keeps the direction Carl's, but the story would WAIT for
   input: a visitor who only watches sees it stop. Kept regardless: **each section should hold attention on its own.**
2. **The camera drifts a few degrees toward the cursor and settles back** (the shots and the timing unchanged). Fits a directed
   camera as an offset on top; must not fight a speed ramp; no cursor on phones. Echoes the logo "noticing the visitor".
3. **Skip to the end if they scroll away.** Conflicts with 23 November (the hero FREEZES when scrolled away, restarts after a
   navigation); could fit as what a RETURNING visitor sees (the gold on marble, not a frozen mid-pass). Changes what the visitor
   sees: Carl's before anything is built.
